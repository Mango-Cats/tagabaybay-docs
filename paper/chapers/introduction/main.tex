\section{Introduction}

Borrowing words from one language (or the source language \( L_2 \)) to another
(the borrowing language \( L_1 \)) is a common linguistic phenomenon. For
English, the word \emph{modus operandi} may be used in criminal investigations.
In the context of shipping, one may use \emph{cruise}, \emph{dock}, or
\emph{yacht}, all of which are from Dutch.

Borrowing is more common in locales where multiple languages are being used
simultaneously in similar contexts. An example of this is the Philippine
context: which has both Filipino (\( \mathcal{F} \)) and English (\(
\mathcal{E} \)) as their official language. In casual contexts, like
going to school, one may use \emph{paaralan} which is the native Filipino
word for ``school''. Or borrow by using \emph{eskuwelahan} from Spanish, or
\emph{iskul} or \emph{skul} from English. Furthermore, the Philippines also has
a rich history of borrowings from Malay and Chinese way before they started
borrowing from Spanish and English (during their colonial
period)~\cite{Baklanova_2017}.

In general, \( L_1 \) borrows a word \( w \in L_2 \), we consider how the \( w \) is
pronounced and written in \( L_2 \) and constraints in \( L_1
\)~\cite{Uffman_2015,Mao_Hulden_2016}. Using the example above, we look at the
base pronunciation and spelling of \emph{school}, which are \textipa{/sku:l/}
and ``school''. With the relevant Filipino constraints: ``i/e-'' prefix for
English or Spanish loanwords and strict phoneme-grapheme mapping. Which is why
the adapted loanword is \emph{iskul}.

The algorithmic task of transforming \( w \in L_2 \) to its adapted form \(
\omega \in \mathcal{F} \) has importance in Filipino-centric NLP where loanword
adaptation or code-switching occurs, like spell and grammar checking for
Filipino text data with loanwords which is common in a most contexts such as
social media posts, casual conversations, and technical settings where using
the native words may be difficult. Or, text-to-speech or sentence generation
for Taglish.

Finally, even though Filipino has a orthography manual publicly available,
no software solution is yet to be implemented for writing adapted loanwords.

Hence, the goal of the project is to study loanword adaptation in Filipino
and implementing an adaptation algorithm.
