\section{Results and Discussion}

The system was evaluated on 2,319 English--Filipino loanword pairs.
\Cref{tab:CER} reports overall CER under four vowel-equivalence conditions,
comparing the proposed IPA-guided system against a baseline that maps each
vowel to itself without G2P or phonetic rules.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Overall CER (\%) under four vowel-equivalence conditions.}
    \label{tab:CER}
    \begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
        \hline
        \textbf{System} & \textbf{5-vowel} & \textbf{e=i=y} & \textbf{o=u} & \textbf{3-vowel} \\
        \hline
        Baseline        & 17.73            & 14.50          & 17.42        & 14.19            \\
        IPA-guided      & 5.15             & 4.14           & 5.06         & 4.05             \\
        \hline
        Reduction       & 12.58            & 10.36          & 12.36        & 10.14            \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Under strict five-vowel evaluation, the IPA-guided system achieved 5.15\% CER,
a 71\% relative reduction from the 17.73\% baseline. Relaxing (\emph{e},
\emph{i}, \emph{y}) equivalence reduced CER to 4.14\%, while (\emph{o},
\emph{u}) relaxation yielded 5.06\%. Combining both equivalences produced
4.05\% CER. The larger improvement from \emph{e}/\emph{i}/\emph{y} relaxation
versus \emph{o}/\emph{u} reflects the prevalence of schwa-derived ambiguity:
English unstressed vowels reduce to /\textipa{@}/, which can map to any of the
five vowels.

\subsection{Error Analysis}

Manual analysis of the errors under 3-vowel evaluation reveals 3 primary
failure modes.

\subsubsection{Unstressed vowel ambiguity}

The single largest error source involves the mapping of English unstressed
vowels, the schwa /\textipa{@}/, to Filipino. Because Filipino orthography
lacks a dedicated schwa character, every unstressed vowel must be resolved to
one of the five target vowels \emph{a}, \emph{e}, \emph{i}, \emph{o}, \emph{u}.

\subsubsection{G2P Propagation Errors}

When the G2P module produces an incorrect intermediate phonemic representation,
the error cascades into the final output.For example, ``chlorpromazine''
produces ``kloroporamasin'' rather than the expected ``klorpromasin'', where
the consonant cluster /pr/ is erroneously broken by a spurious vowel.

\subsubsection{Conventional loanword divergence}

A subset of errors reflects cases where established Filipino borrowing
conventions override phonetically systematic predictions. The most prominent is
the \emph{f} \( \mapsto \) \emph{p} substitution. Filipino historically lacked /f/, and many established
loanwords preserve the older /p/ mapping.

\subsection{Limitations}

Three limitations warrant consideration. First, rules derived from prescriptive
sources may not capture informal nativization patterns in social media or
colloquial text. Second, the rule set is Filipino-specific; porting to other
languages requires new linguistic expertise. Third, performance is bounded by
G2P accuracy—phonemization errors propagate to final output, as demonstrated by
the ``air conditioner'' example.
