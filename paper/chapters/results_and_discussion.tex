\section{Results and Discussion}

The system was evaluated on 2,319 English-Filipino loanword pairs.
\Cref{tab:CER} reports overall CER under four vowel-equivalence conditions. 

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Overall CER (\%) under four vowel-equivalence conditions.}
    \label{tab:CER}
    \begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
        \hline
        \textbf{System}           & \textbf{5-vowel} & \textbf{(\gr{e}, \gr{i}, \gr{y})} & \textbf{(\gr{o}, \gr{u})} & \textbf{3-vowel} \\
        \hline
        Baseline                  & 20.38            & 17.17                                   & 20.04                         & 16.83            \\
        Ortho-only                & 17.73            & 14.50                                   & 17.42                         & 14.19            \\
        IPA-guided                & 5.14             & 4.14                                    & 5.05                          & 4.05             \\
        LLM Prompting & 15.54            & 13.39                                   & 15.29                         & 13.14            \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The IPA-guided system achieved 5.14\% CER under strict five-vowel evaluation,
a 12.59-point reduction from the 17.73\% ortho-only ablation and a
15.24-point reduction from the 20.38\% identity baseline. LLM
prompting achieved 15.54\% CER, outperforming the identity baseline but
underperforming the ortho-only ablation. This result indicates that even with
explicit nativization rules in the prompt, an LLM cannot reliably apply them at
the character level, and that the rewrite cascade substantially
outperforms prompting-based approaches for this task.
Under (\gr{e}, \gr{i}, \gr{y}) equivalence, CER fell to 4.14\%; under
(\gr{o}, \gr{u}) equivalence it yielded 5.05\%; the combination of both
produced 4.05\%. The larger gain from (\gr{e}, \gr{i}, \gr{y})
equivalence reflects two overlapping sources of vowel ambiguity: schwa
resolution and (\gr{i}, \gr{y}) alternation in glide contexts. The marginal
effect of (\gr{o}, \gr{u}) equivalence confirms that
this alternation is rare in the evaluation data.

\subsection{Error Analysis}

Manual inspection of the 856 character-level edits under 3-vowel scoring
reveals four dominant failure modes.

\subsubsection{Unstressed Vowel Ambiguity}

Schwa resolution is the largest error source. Under 3-vowel evaluation,
vowel-to-vowel substitutions dominate: \gr{a}~$\mapsto$~\gr{o} accounts for
46 errors, \gr{e}~$\mapsto$~\gr{a} for 37, \gr{o}~$\mapsto$~\gr{a} for
27, and \gr{a}~$\mapsto$~\gr{e} for 23, which total over 133 vowel
substitutions. The (\gr{a}, \gr{o}) contrast persists even after (\gr{e},
\gr{i}, \gr{y}) and (\gr{o}, \gr{u}) are collapsed, so this ambiguity
is structural and requires lexical stress information to resolve.

\subsubsection{Incomplete Glide Insertion}

Filipino phonotactics requires glides /w/ and /j/ to break vowel hiatus. The
current rules miss 42 \gr{w}-insertion and 40 \gr{y}-insertion sites under
5-vowel evaluation. ``tiara'' maps to ``tiara'' instead of the expected
``tiyara''. Additional context conditions for vowel-initial suffixes and
word-final sequences would close these gaps.

\subsubsection{G2P Propagation Errors}

Some failures originate in the upstream G2P module rather than in the rewrite
rules. An incorrect IPA transcription directly affects   the output:
``chlorpromazine'' yields ``klor\textbf{o}p\textbf{o}r\textbf{a}masin'' instead
of ``klorpromasin''. 40 of the edits trace to incorrect G2P
transcriptions, and these concentrate in pharmaceutical terms and long compound
words where eSpeak-NG's accuracy degrades. A specialized pronunciation lexicon
would directly reduce this error class.

\subsubsection{Conventional Loanword Divergence}

Established borrowing conventions override phonetic predictions in 19
\gr{f}~$\mapsto$~\gr{p} errors. Filipino historically lacked /f/, and
entrenched loanwords preserve the older bilabial mapping. The system applies
modern rules that recognize /f/ in the extended alphabet and therefore retains
the fricative in all positions.

\subsection{Limitations}

Three limitations warrant consideration. First, the rules derive from
prescriptive sources and may not capture informal nativization patterns.
Second, the rule set is Filipino-specific, though the pipeline architecture,
multigraph tokenizer, and rewrite cascade are language-general; only the rule
inventory, phoneme-to-grapheme mappings, and normalization repairs require
re-engineering per target language. Third, G2P accuracy bounds overall
performance, as phonemization errors propagate directly to the output.
