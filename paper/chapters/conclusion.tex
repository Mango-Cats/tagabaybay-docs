\section{Conclusion}
 
We presented a rule-based rewrite cascade for orthographic nativization of 
English loanwords into Filipino, demonstrating that prescriptive linguistic 
rules can be operationalized into an effective computational system without 
parallel training data. The four-stage pipeline---graphemic tokenization, 
priority-ordered rule application, phonetic vowel resolution, and output 
normalization---achieved 5.14\% CER under strict five-vowel evaluation and 4.05\% 
when accounting for Filipino's allographic vowel variation.
 
Error analysis revealed that most remaining errors stem from schwa-derived 
vowel ambiguity, where unstressed syllables reduce to /\textipa{@}/ and map 
ambiguously to multiple Filipino vowel letters. This limitation suggests two 
promising directions for future work. First, incorporating prosodic information 
or lexical stress patterns could improve vowel mapping accuracy by 
distinguishing stressed versus unstressed contexts. Second, a hybrid approach 
combining rule-based foundations with data-driven refinement of ambiguous cases 
could leverage the interpretability of linguistic rules while addressing edge 
cases through corpus statistics.
 
The framework's transparency and linguistic grounding make it particularly 
suitable for low-resource scenarios where annotated loanword corpora are 
unavailable. Future work will explore extending the approach to other 
typologically diverse language pairs and evaluating performance on informal 
register text, where nativization patterns may diverge from prescriptive 
orthographic conventions.
