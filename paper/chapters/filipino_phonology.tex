\section{Filipino Orthographic Conventions for Borrowed Vocabulary}\label{sec:filipino_orthography_and_phonology}

This section describes the spelling conventions and orthographic properties of
Filipino that govern how English loanwords are nativized in writing.

\subsection{Vowel Spelling and Allographic Variation}

Filipino orthography uses five vowel letters: \emph{a}, \emph{e}, \emph{i},
\emph{o}, and \emph{u}~\cite{almario2014}. Because English distinguishes a much
larger set of vowel sounds, multiple English vowels must collapse onto a single
Filipino letter when a word is nativized. For instance, English /\textipa{\ae}/
and /\textipa{A:}/ are both written as \emph{a} in Filipino.

The pairs \emph{e}/\emph{i} and \emph{o}/\emph{u} are widely treated as
interchangeable in many lexical contexts, a convention rooted in the historical
allophonic status of these pairs in Tagalog~\cite{schachter1972}. In practice,
native speakers often accept either spelling variant without perceiving an
error. This variation is especially common in borrowed vocabulary, where the
choice between them is not always uniquely prescribed.

\subsection{Consonant Letter Correspondences}

For the fifteen consonant letters of the native Filipino
alphabet~\cite{llamzon1966}, spelling correspondences between English and
Filipino are largely straightforward. However, English loanwords frequently
contain sounds that have no native Filipino letter, including /f/, /v/, /z/,
/\textipa{S}/, /\textipa{tS}/, and /\textipa{dZ}/. But there are standard
written forms for these cases: /\textipa{tS}/ is written as \emph{ts},
/\textipa{dZ}/ as \emph{dy}, and /\textipa{S}/ as \emph{s} or
\emph{sy}~\cite{almario2014,llamzon1966}.

\subsection{Syllable-Level Spelling Repairs}

Filipino spelling strongly favors syllable patterns of the form CV or
CVC~\cite{llamzon1966}. When a nativized spelling would produce a consonant
cluster that violates these patterns, a
vowel letter is inserted to break up the illegal sequence, preserving all
source consonants in the spelling~\cite{almario2014}.
