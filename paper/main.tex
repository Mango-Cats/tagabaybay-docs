\begin{abstract}
Orthographic nativization, generating standardized written forms of loanwords in 
a recipient language, remains understudied computationally despite its importance 
for text normalization and multilingual NLP. We present a rule-based rewrite 
cascade for orthographic nativization of English loanwords into Filipino. The 
system operates through four stages: graphemic tokenization, priority-ordered 
rule application, phonetic vowel resolution via Grapheme-to-Phoneme lookup, and 
output normalization. Rules are hand-authored from prescriptive orthographic 
guidelines and descriptive phonological sources, enabling deployment without 
parallel training corpora. We evaluate on a newly constructed gold standard of 
2,319 English--Filipino loanword pairs. The system achieves 5.14\% character error rate 
under strict evaluation and 4.05\% when accounting for Filipino's allographic 
vowel variation. Error analysis reveals that most remaining errors stem from 
schwa ambiguity in unstressed syllables, suggesting directions for phonetically 
informed refinement. The linguistically grounded approach demonstrates that 
prescriptive rules can be operationalized into effective nativization systems 
for low-resource languages.
\end{abstract}
