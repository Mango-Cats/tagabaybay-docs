\begin{abstract}

    Orthographic nativization, generating standardized written forms of loanwords,
    remains understudied computationally despite its importance for text
    normalization and multilingual NLP. We present a rule-based approach for
    orthographic nativization of English loanwords into Filipino. The system
    operates through multiple stages: graphemic tokenization, priority-ordered
    rewrite rules, and grapheme-to-phoneme lookup. Rules are hand-authored from
    orthographic guidelines and phonological sources, enabling deployment without
    parallel training corpora. We evaluate on a newly constructed gold standard of
    2,319 English--Filipino loanword pairs. The proposed method achieves 5.14\%
    error rate under a 5-vowel and 4.05\% under a 3-vowel evaluation. The system
    outperforms a regular expressions and zero-shot prompting of a large language model given
    the same mapping rules. Error analysis reveals that most remaining errors stem
    from schwa ambiguity in unstressed syllables, glide insertion, and the quality
    of the phonetic transcription. The linguistically grounded approach
    demonstrates that prescriptive rules can be operationalized into effective
    orthographic nativization systems for low-resource languages.

\end{abstract}
